Engineering / Structural Optimization

Atmospheric Water Harvester: Structural Quad-Pod System

UT FIRE Program / Dr. Guihua Yu Materials Laboratory • Jan - Apr 2026

Dr. Guihua Yu and Yaxuan Zhao's residence-scale water harvester extracts potable water from ambient air using hydrogel sheets that passively absorb moisture overnight and release water vapor when heated by solar concentrators during the day.

The Engineering Challenge

The original laboratory rig relied on 6mm laser-cut acrylic sheets and slip-fit 3D-printed joints. The apparatus was brittle, lacked torsional rigidity, could not adapt to uneven outdoor terrain, and risked catastrophic tipping under wind loads acting against its 30 lb payload and wide, tilted casing.

Design & Fabrication Decisions
  • Splayed Quad-Pod Geometry: Modeled a rigid frame in Onshape with legs angled at 20 degrees in both lateral and longitudinal planes. This created a 27.2 degree true compound angle, widening the base stance from 21 cm to 34 cm (a 62% increase) and vastly raising tipping resistance.
  • Threaded Rod Architecture: Replaced the acrylic legs with four 250mm M10 x 1.5mm pitch 304 stainless steel threaded rods to prevent column buckling under the payload. Terminated each leg with heavy-duty rubberized M10 leveling feet with a 30-degree pivot for rough terrain grip.
  • Direct Load-Transfer Hub: 3D-printed a central head block on a Raise3D Pro3 using structural PLA (5 shell layers, 6 solid top layers, 18% gyroid infill). Hexagonal pockets encapsulated 30mm M10 coupling nuts via press-fit and cyanoacrylate, directing compressive forces directly into the steel rods rather than relying on plastic friction.
  • Lower Incline Standoff: Designed a secondary 3D-printed foot block at the base of the hydrogel case as a positive stop, preventing slippage along its 30-degree operating incline.
Outcome & Impact

The redesigned quad-pod increased base stability by 62%, eliminated wind tipping vulnerability during field testing, and disassembled into a compact bundle for rugged transport.

Robotics & Vehicle Systems

Scratch-Engineered 1:10 Scale RC Vehicle

Independent Engineering Project • Dec 2025 - Present

Built from a clean CAD sheet rather than an off-the-shelf kit to master suspension kinematics, mechanical tolerancing, and real-time embedded communications.

Chassis & Kinematics Design

Modeled the full chassis, multi-link double-wishbone suspension, and steering linkages in Onshape. Applied mechanical ball-and-tangent mates to simulate travel over the full stroke and tuned steering geometry to eliminate bump steer under hard compression.

Additive Manufacturing & Stress Vectors

Load-bearing suspension arms, steering knuckles, and motor mounts were rapid-prototyped on FDM printers. Print layer lines were oriented parallel to tensile and shear planes to prevent delamination during high-velocity impacts.

Embedded Electronics & ESP-NOW Control
  • Telemetry Link: Replaced commercial RC receivers with two ESP32 microcontrollers communicating over ESP-NOW, achieving sub-10ms transmission latency.
  • Control Pipeline: An ESP32 host processes analog stick inputs from an Xbox controller and transmits packets to an onboard receiver.
  • Firmware Tuning: Authored custom PWM generation code to drive an 80A electronic speed controller (ESC) and metal-gear servo, mapping non-linear throttle curves and software deadzones.
Mechatronics & Rapid Prototyping

The Borrow Box: Automatic Classroom Supply Dispenser

UT SEC Makeathon • Most Technical Design Award (1st of 18 teams)

Designed and built in 48 hours to eliminate classroom disruptions when students borrow pencils, providing a frictionless, verifiable checkout system for teachers.

Mechanical & Sensor Architecture
  • Gravity-Fed Escapement: An angled intake chute directs pencils into a servo-actuated rotating barrel that reliably isolates and dispenses one pencil at a time without motorized pushers.
  • Optical Token Identification: Integrated a color sensor that scans student-assigned color tokens to authenticate checkout transactions.
  • At-A-Glance Status Array: Built a hardware LED grid indicating which student slots currently hold an unreturned pencil, giving teachers an immediate status check without apps or screens.
Critical Pivot Under Competition Pressure

Testing revealed two points of failure: pencils bridged in the wide chute, and 3D-printed barrel tolerances bound under friction. With hours left, we narrowed the chute geometry to force single-file alignment and redesigned the rotating drum in Onshape to be laser-cut from clear acrylic, slashing friction and meeting live judging on time.

Thermal & Fluid Product Design

Active Deodorizing Laundry Hamper

Texas Inventionworks Design Challenge • Oct 2025 - Present

Traditional laundry hampers trap stagnant, humid air, fostering bacterial growth and persistent odor. This project introduced an active airflow enclosure that dries damp clothing before odors set in.

Airflow Architecture & Joinery

Modeled in SolidWorks with interlocking finger joints to fit inside 24" x 48" laser-cutter sheets. An intake fan at the base forces air upward through waffle-pattern grates, aligning with natural thermal convection to vent moisture out the top.

Design Simplification Through Testing

Early prototypes included activated carbon exhaust filters. Quantitative testing demonstrated that continuous air displacement prevented volatile organic compounds (VOCs) from concentrating in the first place. Eliminating the filter cut user maintenance costs, reduced static pressure, and allowed the fan to run silently.
